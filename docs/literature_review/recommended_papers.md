# Literature Review: Wikipedia Dispute Resolution

Papers organized by relevance to the project's goals: mapping dispute emergence, escalation, and resolution across Wikipedia's five-stage intervention framework.

---

## Core Papers: Wikipedia Conflict & Governance

### 1. Edit Wars in Wikipedia
**Kittur, A., Suh, B., Pendleton, B. A., & Chi, E. H. (2007)**
*Proceedings of CHI 2007*
DOI: `10.1145/1240624.1240698`

Foundational analysis of edit wars on Wikipedia. Introduces metrics for detecting conflict through revert patterns. Directly relevant to arbitration case analysis and edit war mapping.

---

### 2. Cooperation and Conflict between Authors with History
**Kittur, A., & Kraut, R. E. (2010)**
*Proceedings of CHI 2010*
DOI: `10.1145/1753326.1753440`

Examines how prior interaction history between editors affects subsequent collaboration quality. Useful for modeling dispute escalation patterns.

---

### 3. The Rise and Decline of an Open Collaboration System
**Halfaker, A., Geiger, R. S., Morgan, J. T., & Riedl, J. (2013)**
*American Behavioral Scientist, 57(5)*
DOI: `10.1177/0002764212469365`

Documents Wikipedia's increasingly hostile environment for newcomers. Relevant for understanding how dispute resolution mechanisms evolved and their effects on editor retention.

---

### 4. Don't Bite the Newbies
**Halfaker, A., Kittur, A., & Riedl, J. (2011)**
*Proceedings of WikiSym 2011*
DOI: `10.1145/2038558.2038585`

Analyzes how experienced editors treat newcomers and the role of reverts in driving away contributors. Connects conduct issues to community health.

---

## Dispute Resolution Mechanisms

### 5. The Work of Sustaining Order in Wikipedia
**Geiger, R. S., & Ribes, D. (2010)**
*Proceedings of CSCW 2010*
DOI: `10.1145/1718918.1718941`

Ethnographic study of Wikipedia's anti-vandalism infrastructure. Documents how bots, tools, and human editors collaborate in governance—relevant for understanding the human-automated systems interplay in dispute detection.

---

### 6. Governance Through Gatekeeping: Wikipedia's Deletion System
**Taraborelli, D., & Ciampaglia, G. L. (2010)**
*WikiSym 2010*

Analyzes Articles for Deletion (AfD) discussions as a governance mechanism. Useful parallel for understanding how consensus emerges in contentious decisions.

---

### 7. When the Levee Breaks: Without Bots, What Happens to Wikipedia's Quality Control Processes?
**Geiger, R. S., & Halfaker, A. (2013)**
*WikiSym 2013*
DOI: `10.1145/2491055.2491061`

Examines bot infrastructure supporting Wikipedia governance. Critical for understanding automated components of dispute detection and response.

---

## Network Analysis & Conflict Detection

### 8. Detecting Conflict in Online Social Networks
**Kumar, S., Spezzano, F., Subrahmanian, V. S., & Faloutsos, C. (2014)**
*IEEE International Conference on Data Mining (ICDM)*
DOI: `10.1109/ICDM.2014.87`

Proposes methods for detecting conflict between user pairs in collaborative platforms. Applicable to modeling editor disputes before formal escalation.

---

### 9. Antisocial Behavior in Online Discussion Communities
**Cheng, J., Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil, C., & Leskovec, J. (2015)**
*Proceedings of ICWSM 2015*

Studies how antisocial users behave before and after being banned. Relevant for understanding conduct violations that lead to arbitration.

---

### 10. Social Roles and Processes in Wikipedia
**Welser, H. T., Cosley, D., Kossinets, G., Lin, A., Dokshin, F., Gay, G., & Smith, M. (2011)**
*American Behavioral Scientist, 55(10)*
DOI: `10.1177/0002764211414601`

Identifies distinct editor roles through behavioral signatures. Useful for characterizing disputants and mediators.

---

## Natural Language Processing for Conflict

### 11. A Large-Scale Empirical Study of Conflict in Wikipedia Discussions
**Kittur, A., Chi, E., Pendleton, B., Suh, B., & Mytkowicz, T. (2007)**
*WikiSym 2007*

Develops NLP methods for detecting conflict in talk page discussions. Directly applicable to analyzing DRN and arbitration case text.

---

### 12. Conversations Gone Awry: Detecting Early Signs of Conversational Failure
**Zhang, J., Chang, J., Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil, C., Dixon, L., Hua, Y., Taraborelli, D., & Thain, N. (2018)**
*Proceedings of ACL 2018*
DOI: `10.18653/v1/P18-1125`

Develops models predicting whether a conversation will derail into personal attacks. Uses Wikipedia Talk Page Conversations corpus. Highly relevant for early conflict detection.

---

### 13. Characterizing and Detecting Hateful Users on Twitter
**Ribeiro, M. H., Calais, P. H., Santos, Y. A., Almeida, V. A., & Meira Jr, W. (2018)**
*ICWSM 2018*

Methods for detecting harmful behavior patterns. Applicable to identifying problematic editors before arbitration.

---

## Consensus & Decision-Making

### 14. The Dynamics of Peer-Produced Political Information
**Shi, F., Teplitskiy, M., Duede, E., & Evans, J. A. (2019)**
*Nature Human Behaviour, 3*
DOI: `10.1038/s41562-019-0592-2`

Analyzes how politically controversial Wikipedia articles achieve neutral content through repeated editing. Relevant for understanding content dispute resolution outcomes.

---

### 15. Computational Analysis of the Historical Changes in Topic-Specific Sentiment on Wikipedia
**Rodi, G. C., Loreto, V., & Tria, F. (2019)**
*Digital Scholarship in the Humanities*

Tracks sentiment evolution in Wikipedia articles. Methods applicable to measuring dispute resolution effectiveness.

---

## Datasets & Tools

### 16. WikiTalk Corpus / Wikipedia Talk Pages Corpus
**Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al.**
Data: https://convokit.cornell.edu/

Annotated dataset of Wikipedia talk page conversations with toxicity labels. Essential for training conflict detection models.

---

### 17. Wikipedia Edit History Dataset
**Wikimedia Foundation**
Data: https://dumps.wikimedia.org/

Complete revision histories. Source for revert analysis and edit war detection.

---

### 18. The Wikipedia Edit Wars Dataset
**Yasseri, T., Sumi, R., Rung, A., Kornai, A., & Kertész, J. (2012)**
*PLoS ONE*
DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0038869`

Quantifies edit wars across Wikipedia using a mutually reverting edit index. Methods directly applicable to this project.

---

## Governance & Policy

### 19. The Life and Death of Online Groups: Predicting Group Growth and Longevity
**Kairam, S., Wang, D. J., & Leskovec, J. (2012)**
*WSDM 2012*
DOI: `10.1145/2124295.2124374`

Analyzes growth patterns in online communities. Framework applicable to understanding how dispute resolution venues evolve.

---

### 20. WikiProject Governance: Collective and Distributed Decision-Making
**Forte, A., & Bruckman, A. (2008)**
*WikiSym 2008*
DOI: `10.1145/1461477.1461481`

Studies how WikiProjects self-govern. Relevant for understanding dispute resolution at the project level.
